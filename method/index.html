Method

Six commitments.

They hold whatever the subject is. What changes between a person, a company and a supply chain is which registers apply — not how a finding is established, and not what we do when it cannot be.

01
Resolve the subject before searching

The common failure in this work is not missing a finding. It is attaching a real finding to the wrong person or the wrong company, which is worse than returning nothing, because it looks like diligence. So the first work is establishing who or what the subject actually is, and every later search is anchored to that.

02
Every finding carries its source and its date

Each entry links to the record it came from, and is dated by the underlying event rather than by when an article about it was published. We hold no proprietary database, which means we cannot ask you to take our word for anything — you can open every source and check our reading of it. That is a stronger position than owning the data.

03
Nothing is inferred

A signal that identifies a subject is not a signal that says anything against them. Identity resolution, profile matching and document comparison are recorded because they tell you how confident the identification is — and they never move a score. Neither does a weak name match, which is surfaced for a reviewer and left there.

04
Coverage is stated

Every report names the sources it reached and the sources it did not, and why. A jurisdiction outside our registry coverage, a profile that could not be retrieved, a document that could not be read — each is named. The absence of a finding from a source that was never queried is not a clean result, and we will not let a report read as though it were.

05
A person releases every report

No assessment is delivered automatically. Before release, an adjudication pass reviews the findings and can only remove or downgrade them — it cannot add a finding, raise one, or release anything. Then a person reads the report and signs it. This is a control on the automation. It is not an exemption from any regulatory obligation, and we do not describe it as one.

06
Where the record stops is itself a finding

The point at which traceability ends carries information: about the structure being examined, and about how much weight the rest of the assessment can bear. It is reported with a severity, in the body of the work, not softened into a footnote at the end.

What this is not

The limits, stated once.

We do not make a recommendation. An assessment sets out what the record establishes; the decision, and the judgement that goes into it, stay with you. A report that shades into advice is no longer an assessment.

We do not contact employers, referees or institutions, and we do not verify employment or education. Documents are compared against each other and against public records; where they disagree, we say so and show both.

We do not assess creditworthiness or financial standing, and we do not offer ongoing monitoring. An assessment describes a subject as at a stated date.

We do not use non-public data. Everything in a Previo report was publicly available at the time it was collected.
