The decision this serves

The name on the contract
is almost never the answer.

A company register tells you who is formally registered. It does not tell you who ends up holding the economic interest once a holding company owns a holding company — and that is the question every sanctions, procurement and AML obligation actually turns on.

A single-layer check reads a holding company as the owner and stops. Previo does not stop there.

What runs

Five stages, in order.

01
Corporate standing

Registration status and filing history, and the structural signals that carry meaning on their own: insolvency, compulsory dissolution, strike-off — and the absence of any currently registered owner, which is a finding in itself rather than a blank.

02
Sanctions on the entity

The company screened against the consolidated OFAC, UN, UK OFSI and EU lists, with weak matches surfaced for a reviewer rather than resolved in silence.

03
Adverse media on the entity

Published reporting against the company in English and Danish, dated by the underlying event and discounted where the match to the entity is weak.

04
Ownership traced to real people

The chain is walked upward through holding companies until it reaches natural persons, with stakes multiplied along the way — so a third of a holding company that wholly owns the subject reads as a third of the subject, which is the number your obligations actually attach to.

05
The people behind it, screened

Owners, beneficial owners and directors screened in turn — up to ten of them, prioritised by control rather than by title — and one bounded step out into the other companies they run.

How the output reads

One band,
for any company.

A company is scored on a flat scale — it has no seniority to adjust for. Its own registration status is the dominant signal; findings against the people behind it are weighted by how close they sit to control.

No material findings against the entity or the people behind it.

50–79

0–49

Insolvency, compulsory dissolution, a sanctions match, or serious regulatory action.
