The decision this serves

The name on the contract
is rarely the answer.

A company register tells you who is formally registered. It does not tell you who ends up holding the economic interest once a holding company owns a holding company, and that is usually the question worth asking.

We built this after screening our own group and missing our own owner, because the subject was held through a parent. Ownership piercing exists because that failure is easy and quiet.

What runs

Five stages, in order.

01
Corporate standing

Registration status, filing history and the structural signals that carry meaning on their own: insolvency, compulsory dissolution, strike-off, and the absence of any currently registered owner, which is a transparency gap rather than a neutral fact.

02
Sanctions on the entity

The company itself screened against the consolidated lists, with weak matches surfaced for a reviewer rather than resolved silently.

03
Adverse media on the entity

Published reporting against the company, dated by the underlying event, discounted where the match to the entity is weak.

04
Ownership traced to natural persons

The ownership chain is walked upward through holding companies until it reaches people. Stakes are multiplied along the chain, so a third of a holding company that wholly owns the subject reads as a third — not as a third of nothing. The walk is bounded, and every point where it stopped is reported.

05
The people behind it, screened

Owners, beneficial owners and directors are screened in turn, and one bounded step is taken into the other companies they run. That step can raise a caution on its own but is capped so it can never, by itself, produce the lowest band.

The source register

Every source, and what it can establish.

Source What it establishes Coverage
Company register Registration status, officers, and the registered ownership layer UK, Denmark
Ownership chain The natural persons at the top of a holding structure, with stakes multiplied along the chain Denmark

Where it stops

Bounded, and it says
where the bound fell.

The ownership walk runs on Danish records. A UK structure is traced through its registered ownership and parent filings, which is a shallower instrument, and the report distinguishes the two rather than presenting one depth.

Registry coverage is the United Kingdom and Denmark. A company registered anywhere else is screened against sanctions and published reporting only, and its corporate standing is reported as not screened — not as clean.

The walk is depth- and lookup-bounded. When a bound is reached, when a lookup fails, or when a register holds no current owner, that is written into the report at the point it happened. An ownership chain that ends because we ran out of budget looks different from one that ends because there is nothing above it, and the report never conflates them.

A fixed number of directors and owners are screened, prioritised by control rather than by title. Where the roster was truncated, the report says how many were left.

How the output reads

One band, for any company.

A company is scored on a flat scale with no role adjustment, because a company has no seniority. The entity's own registration status is the dominant signal; findings against the people behind it are weighted by how close they sit to control, and the network step is capped.
