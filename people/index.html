The decision this serves

Fit and proper,
with evidence.

The question is rarely whether a candidate looks right in an interview. It is whether there is anything in the public record that a regulator, a journalist or a board will find later, and whether you can show you went and looked before you appointed.

Previo answers both. Every finding dated and linked to the record it came from, and an audit trail that shows the check was run, when, and against what.

The second pass

Then we look at
who they do it with.

A first pass finds what is filed against a name. The interesting structures are rarely filed against the name you were given.

Where the first pass surfaces a spouse, a business partner or a family member with a material connection, Previo runs a second, cross-referenced pass over them. That is the standard vector for nominee arrangements and for value parked one step away from the subject. It is the part manual vetting almost never reaches.

How the output reads

A score you can
take apart.

The report is the product. The number is the shorthand.

A subject begins at 100 and every deduction is itemised, so you can trace the number to the finding and the finding to the source. Thresholds move with the seniority of the role: a public-office appointment clears at a higher score than an individual contributor. The bands below are the default rather than the scale.

80–100
Clear

No material findings. Released after review, like every report.

50–79
Caution

Findings that warrant a conversation with the candidate before a decision.

0–49
Do not proceed

Sanctions matches, serious regulatory action, or a pattern incompatible with the role.

Consent and lawful basis

Clean to run,
and to defend.

The candidate receives a secure invitation and gives consent before anything runs, and consent is revocable at any point before release. Processing is conducted under GDPR Article 6(1)(f), legitimate interest, with a Legitimate Interests Assessment available on request and subject access requests handled as a matter of course.

Retention matches your regulatory obligations. The audit trail travels with the report, which is what you want when someone asks, two years later, what you knew and when.
